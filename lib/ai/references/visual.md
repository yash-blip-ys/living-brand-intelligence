# Visual direction

- Build a visual system from brand behavior, not from a mood adjective alone. Translate positioning and personality into composition, typography, shape, color, and imagery decisions.
- Choose a functional hierarchy: what must be understood first, what supports it, and what can be removed. Consistency comes from repeatable relationships, not identical decoration.
- Typography should establish reading order and tone. Define display and text roles, weight behavior, and density rather than naming fonts without evidence.
- Color direction should state role, proportion, contrast, and accessibility intent. Do not rely on color alone to communicate status.
- Imagery should reflect the real audience and product context. Avoid generic stock photography, decorative gradients, and visual clichés that contradict the position.
- Document concepts to avoid so the system remains coherent across generated assets and future contributors.

Example direction: “An editorial grid with one decisive accent color, generous spacing, and product imagery that shows real work rather than lifestyle aspiration.”
