# Voice and personality

- Personality describes stable brand traits; voice describes how those traits appear in language. Keep both consistent with the audience and positioning.
- Define observable behavior. Instead of “friendly,” specify how the brand welcomes, explains trade-offs, handles mistakes, and treats expert users.
- Use a small set of language principles with do/don’t examples that can be applied across product, marketing, and support.
- Match formality, vocabulary, sentence rhythm, humor, and terminology to the audience. Do not make every touchpoint sound identical when the channel requires adaptation.
- Avoid traits that create friction with accessibility, expertise, cultural context, or high-stakes product use.

Example direction: “Calmly precise: lead with the decision, name the trade-off, and avoid hype or exclamation marks.”
