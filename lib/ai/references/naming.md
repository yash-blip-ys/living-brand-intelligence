# Naming

- Begin with strategic territories, not a list of attractive words. Each territory should connect to positioning, audience needs, personality, or a memorable product truth.
- Favor names that are easy to say, spell, remember, and distinguish from established brands. Pronunciation and retrieval matter as much as abstract meaning.
- Evaluate semantic fit and strategic fit separately. A literal or fashionable name may be memorable but say little about the intended position.
- For every shortlist, record pronunciation, likely misinterpretations, crowded-category risk, domain uncertainty, and trademark uncertainty for later validation.
- Avoid names that overclaim, encode a temporary feature, or force an unnecessary category assumption.

Example direction: A territory such as “evidence made visible” can support names that imply clarity and auditability without claiming a technical mechanism that is not approved evidence.
