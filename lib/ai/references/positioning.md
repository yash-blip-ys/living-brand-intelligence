# Positioning and differentiation

- A strong position names a specific audience, an important job, and a credible alternative or status quo. Avoid category labels that could describe any company.
- Differentiation is a reason to believe, not an adjective. Prefer a product behavior, workflow advantage, audience insight, or trade-off that competitors would find hard to copy.
- State what the brand is, who it is for, and why the chosen approach is preferable. Do not use “the X for Y” as the complete strategy.
- Separate the value promise from the proof. The promise states the outcome; approved context supplies the evidence.
- Test the position with: “Compared with what, for whom, and why does this choice matter?”

Example direction: “Built for small security teams that must produce review-ready evidence without maintaining a full compliance department.”
